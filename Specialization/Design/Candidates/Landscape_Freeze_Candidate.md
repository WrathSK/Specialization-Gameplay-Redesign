## 用户提交原文

# Landscape / 景观专业 — Design Talk

Status:

> **STRONG FREEZE CANDIDATE**
>
> Core philosophy, Lv1–Lv4 ability structure, institution naming direction, operational identity, Management Capacity model, National Park integration, and Network direction are substantially settled.
>
> Exact balance values, some eligibility mappings, and several implementation details remain open.

---

# 1. Specialization Identity

Landscape originates from the Preserve concept, but deliberately rejects the gameplay logic:

> **The less land is developed, the stronger the city becomes.**

The specialization instead represents:

> **limited development, sustainable land use, active landscape creation, long-term land management, formal conservation, and habitat cultivation.**

“Limited development” does **not** mean:

> fewer developed tiles.

It means:

> **no single land-use logic should consume all available space.**

A mature Landscape city may simultaneously contain:

- farms;
- pastures;
- plantations;
- fisheries;
- forests;
- resource extraction;
- ordinary Improvements;
- high-Appeal managed landscapes;
- National Parks;
- urban districts.

The specialization asks:

> **How should land be used over time?**

Development is not treated as a moral mistake.

Conservation is not treated as passive non-action.

Landscape instead tries to make:

> **productive land, managed land, and protected land coexist within one long-term spatial system.**

---

# 2. Progression Philosophy

Landscape follows the same presentation rhythm:

> Lv1 — 0 named abilities  
> Lv2 — 1 named ability  
> Lv3 — 2 named abilities  
> Lv4 — 3 named abilities

Lv1 is not mechanically empty.

It uses a descriptive specialization-foundation section rather than a story-heavy ability name.

The overall progression is:

> **护育 → 营造 → 经营与维护 → 建制、统筹与生境管理**

or conceptually:

> **care → shape → manage → govern**

---

# 3. Lv1 — 风土护育社

Institution:

> **风土护育社**

Status:

> **STRONG CANDIDATE / close to locked**

The Lv1 institution represents a relatively early, local land-stewardship community.

It is intentionally more conservative and protective in tone than later institutions.

This creates an important progression:

> first learn not to damage the land unnecessarily;
>
> later learn to actively shape it.

---

## Specialization Foundation — 基础景观支持

Lv1 has no named ability.

Display label:

> **基础景观支持**

Core philosophy:

> **Necessary development and landscape value can coexist.**

Current mechanic direction:

- eligible resource Improvements that would normally reduce Appeal have that negative effect removed or offset;
- Strategic / Bonus Resources provide Science-oriented compensation;
- Luxury Resources provide Culture-oriented compensation;
- Landscape does not prohibit improving resources.

The purpose is explicitly **not**:

> force the player to leave valuable resources untouched.

Instead:

> development that the player already has strong reasons to perform can be made more compatible with the Landscape specialization.

Exact:

- eligible resource set;
- negative-Appeal treatment;
- compensation values;

remain:

> **BALANCE / MAPPING REQUIRED**

---

# 4. Lv2 — 地景营造所

Institution:

> **地景营造所**

Status:

> **STRONG CANDIDATE / close to locked**

“地景” is preferred over “地貌” because it includes not only physical terrain, but land as shaped through agriculture, fisheries, forestry, resource use, vegetation, and human management.

The institution represents the transition from:

> passive care

to:

> **professional active landscape creation.**

---

## Named Ability — 风貌营造

Status:

> **BASICALLY SETTLED**

Core philosophy:

> **Landscape can be created, not only preserved.**

Current mechanic direction:

- earlier access to tree planting or equivalent Appeal-improvement tools;
- eligible agricultural, fishing, resource, forestry and related Improvements may positively contribute to Appeal;
- good Landscape is not restricted to untouched tiles;
- Improvement does not automatically mean landscape degradation.

Lv2 therefore changes the player’s mental model from:

> “avoid harming good land”

to:

> **“actively make ordinary working land become good landscape.”**

Exact:

- eligible Improvement list;
- Appeal values;
- early-access timing;

remain:

> **BALANCE / MAPPING REQUIRED**

---

# 5. Lv3 — 风物经营局

Institution:

> **风物经营局**

Status:

> **VERY STRONG CANDIDATE / near lock**

“风物” is intentionally broader than scenery alone.

It can include:

- landscape;
- local products;
- managed land;
- place-based economic value.

“经营” also covers both:

- recreation economy;
- long-term land management.

Lv3 is where Landscape becomes a mature operating system rather than only a spatial design philosophy.

---

# 6. Lv3-A — 游憩经济

Status:

> **BASICALLY SETTLED**

The previous two-step design:

- Appeal → Gold;
- Landscape Gold → Tourism;

is now treated as one complete ability.

Core chain:

> **Appeal → Landscape Gold → Tourism**

---

## Stage 1 — Landscape Quality → Local Economic Value

Eligible high-Appeal Landscape Improvements generate Gold.

This Gold represents:

- recreation;
- place-based services;
- environmental amenity value;
- local destination activity;
- landscape-supported economic use.

It is distinct from Commerce.

Commerce creates value through:

> exchange, investment, capital and allocation.

Landscape creates value because:

> **the place itself has become desirable.**

---

## Stage 2 — Local Landscape Economy → Tourism

Gold generated through this Landscape mechanism further contributes Tourism.

The story is:

> a good landscape first supports local recreation and economic activity;
>
> once mature, that place becomes externally recognized and visited.

This deliberately avoids:

> Appeal directly producing a fixed Tourism amount.

Exact:

- high-Appeal threshold;
- Gold formula;
- Landscape Gold → Tourism coefficient;

remain:

> **BALANCE REQUIRED**

---

# 7. Lv3-B — 生态维护

Status:

> **BASICALLY SETTLED MECHANIC / BALANCE PENDING**

Lv3 introduces Landscape’s main operational identity.

It unlocks:

> **景观管护队**

and the active action:

> **生态维护**

---

# 8. 景观管护队

Unit:

> **景观管护队**

Status:

> **STRONG CANDIDATE / close to locked**

This is a dedicated reusable Civilian Unit.

It is deliberately separate from the universal Builder.

Reasons:

- avoid injecting Landscape-specific action logic into a global unit;
- create a visible operational carrier for the specialization;
- allow one unit type to gain more advanced responsibilities at Lv4;
- make Landscape management capacity legible through actual teams.

The unit is not consumed by ordinary maintenance work.

---

# 9. Management Capacity

Landscape uses a city-level:

> **Management Capacity**

This limits both:

1. how many Landscape Management Teams may be trained while current capacity is full;
2. how many Landscape tasks may run simultaneously.

The intended behavior is similar in spirit to Trade Route Capacity:

> capacity is a running-slot system, not merely a production-history counter.

---

## Lv3 Capacity

At ACTIVE III:

> **Base Management Capacity = 1**

Therefore a normal Lv3 Landscape city can operate one Landscape management task at a time.

---

## Capacity and existing units

If current capacity decreases:

- existing excess Landscape Management Teams are **not deleted**;
- new teams cannot be trained while current unit count is at or above capacity;
- only the currently supported number of Landscape tasks may operate.

If capacity loss causes active tasks to exceed capacity:

> excess tasks are interrupted.

Exact deterministic interruption order:

> **TBD**

It must not be random.

---

# 10. Ecological Maintenance — Core Contract

A Landscape Management Team may begin ecological maintenance on:

> an eligible Improvement belonging to its source Landscape city.

Operations are **city-bound**.

A team from City A does not normally maintain City B’s land.

---

## Maintenance period

Current preferred design:

> **The target tile becomes temporarily unworkable by citizens, but its displayed yields are not rewritten.**

During maintenance:

- Terrain remains unchanged;
- Resource remains unchanged;
- Improvement remains unchanged;
- displayed tile yields remain visible;
- citizens cannot work the tile;
- the Landscape Management Team is occupied;
- the task consumes one Management Capacity slot.

This is preferred over directly setting the tile to 0 yield.

The roleplay is:

> the land is resting, being managed, repaired, rotated, maintained or rehabilitated;
>
> it has not ceased to physically exist or produce resources.

---

# 11. Maintenance Completion

After the maintenance period:

- the tile becomes workable again;
- the team becomes available;
- the Improvement receives a permanent mapped-yield increase.

The increase should strengthen:

> **the Improvement’s existing economic role**

rather than add arbitrary unrelated yields.

Conceptual examples:

- Farm → Food
- Mine / Quarry → Production
- Pasture → agricultural Food / Production mapping
- Plantation → appropriate mapped economic yield
- Fishing → Food / Gold
- Lumber Mill → Production

Exact mapping remains:

> **TBD**

---

# 12. Repeatable Maintenance

Ecological Maintenance has:

> **no per-tile maintenance cap.**

The same Improvement may be maintained repeatedly.

This is intentional.

The system avoids:

- “maintained / not maintained” checklist state;
- maintenance levels;
- 1/2 or 2/2 caps;
- arbitrary per-tile lifetime restrictions.

Instead:

> the player decides whether another maintenance cycle is still worth the opportunity cost.

---

# 13. Natural ROI Limiter

The intended balance mechanism is:

> **short-term land-use loss vs permanent long-term improvement.**

A stronger tile costs more to take offline.

If maintenance lasts several turns, each additional maintenance cycle on an already-strong tile naturally has a longer payback period.

Other natural limits include:

- finite remaining game turns;
- limited Management Capacity;
- competition between different tiles;
- competition with other Landscape Management actions.

Therefore:

> infinite theoretical repetition does not imply infinite practical optimal repetition.

Exact maintenance duration and permanent improvement size remain:

> **BALANCE REQUIRED**

Current discussion suggests:

> approximately 5 turns may be a reasonable first test point,

but no value is locked.

The permanent improvement may need to be meaningfully larger than +1 total yield if a normal worked HD tile loses several turns of production.

---

# 14. Unworked Tiles

Unworked eligible Improvements may also receive ecological maintenance.

This is not automatically treated as an exploit.

It represents:

> **future land investment.**

The player may improve a currently unattractive tile in advance so that future population can work it effectively.

Until that tile is actually worked:

> the city does not receive direct yield from the improvement.

The player still spends:

- Management Capacity;
- team time;
- opportunity to maintain another tile.

---

# 15. City Ownership of Maintenance

A Landscape Management Team may start maintenance only on:

> land currently owned by its source city.

If the target tile is reassigned to another city while maintenance is in progress:

> the task loses eligibility and is interrupted.

After maintenance is successfully completed:

> the permanent land improvement remains attached to the plot / Improvement.

If the tile is later reassigned to another city:

> the completed benefit remains.

Unified principle:

> **Landscape operations are city-bound; completed land changes are plot-bound.**

---

# 16. Lv4 — 国家景观统筹署

Institution:

> **国家景观统筹署**

Status:

> **VERY STRONG CANDIDATE / near lock**

Lv4 represents:

> formal protected-area governance;
> larger Landscape management capacity;
> managed buffer landscapes;
> habitat-scale biological cultivation.

A more modern institutional tone is intentional and acceptable here.

---

# 17. Lv4-A — 保护地建制

Status:

> **BASICALLY SETTLED**

Lv4 gains:

- earlier access to Naturalists;
- earlier establishment of National Parks.

This does not mean:

> conservation begins only at Lv4.

Earlier stages already practice sustainable land management.

Lv4 introduces:

> **formal, high-level protected-area institutions.**

---

# 18. National Park → Management Capacity

At ACTIVE IV:

> **each National Park owned by the Landscape city provides +1 Management Capacity.**

Therefore:

> Capacity = 1 + number of National Parks

while the city is ACTIVE IV.

Examples:

- 0 Park → 1
- 1 Park → 2
- 2 Parks → 3

National Park expansion therefore increases:

> how many Landscape operations the city may coordinate simultaneously.

This is not considered a free cap increase.

Each National Park already requires:

- Naturalist investment;
- four tiles;
- strict placement;
- opportunity cost of alternative land use.

This creates a natural soft cap on management intensity.

---

# 19. ACTIVE / Governor Interaction

Management Capacity is a current operating capability.

The current conceptual rule is:

- ACTIVE < III → no Landscape management operation capacity;
- ACTIVE III → base Capacity 1;
- ACTIVE IV → base 1 + National Park contribution.

Landscape Management Teams themselves are trained assets and are not automatically deleted when ACTIVE falls.

However:

> without sufficient current Management Capacity, they cannot continue normal Landscape operations.

If the player voluntarily moves the Governor away:

> the player is accepting the resulting loss of current Landscape operating capability.

The system does not need to protect the player from that choice.

Completed permanent results remain:

- maintained-land upgrades;
- propagated Resources;
- other already-finished land changes.

---

# 20. Lv4-B — 保护区荫庇

Current name:

> **保护区荫庇**

Status:

> **STRONG CANDIDATE / close to settled**

This replaces the earlier working concepts:

- 环带共生;
- 核心荫庇;
- protected-core / buffer synergy.

The name is intentionally simple rather than overly poetic or academic.

---

## Core Mechanic

A National Park acts as:

> **a protected core landscape**

and strengthens nearby:

> **eligible managed Landscape Improvements.**

The real-world conceptual model is:

> protected core + sustainably managed buffer / transition landscape.

The ability does not require a reverse buff from managed land back to the National Park.

---

# 21. Spatial Rules

Current first-test direction:

> **approximately 3-tile range**

from the National Park to eligible managed Landscape Improvements.

2 tiles remain a possible balance fallback.

The design explicitly accepts some topology.

Topology is part of 4X gameplay.

The goal is to avoid:

> topology hell.

Therefore:

- being within range is sufficient;
- bonuses do not scale with number of touching Park tiles;
- multiple Parks do not linearly stack the same effect;
- overlap should use non-stacking / max-style treatment;
- no large taxonomy of adjacency types.

The player should think:

> “Where should my protected core sit relative to my managed landscape?”

not:

> “How many exact hex edges can I optimize?”

> **TBD**

Name:

# 23. Biological Resource Cultivation — Core Contract

A Landscape Management Team selects:

> one eligible native Biological Resource belonging to its source city.

The team then begins a longer cultivation task.

This task:

- occupies one team;
- occupies one Management Capacity;
- lasts longer than ordinary Ecological Maintenance.

Exact duration:

> **TBD**

---

# 24. One Mother, One Child

Each eligible original / native source Resource may successfully propagate:

> **once per lifetime.**

After propagation:

### Mother
becomes permanently:

> propagated / no longer eligible as a source.

### Child
is a real Resource, but:

> permanently cannot propagate again.

Therefore there is no:

> 1 → 2 → 4 → 8

chain.

The ability creates:

> low-frequency map evolution

rather than a Resource production engine.

---

# 25. Propagation Target

The player chooses:

> which mother Resource to cultivate.

The player does **not** directly choose:

> the final destination tile.

The system randomly selects from nearby legal candidate tiles.

Candidates must:

- currently have no Resource;
- satisfy the normal Terrain / Feature legality of that Resource;
- belong to the source Landscape city or otherwise satisfy the final city-bound contract;
- not overwrite another Resource.

A candidate may already contain an Improvement.

Therefore:

> ordinary Improvements cannot cheaply be used to block unwanted candidates.

Players may still influence the candidate pool through real land-use decisions:

- Terrain / Feature manipulation;
- District / Wonder occupation;
- city borders;
- other Resources.

This is considered acceptable Landscape planning rather than automatically an exploit.

---

# 26. Biological Resource Scope

Current direction:

> reuse Harmony in Diversity’s existing **Biological Resource** classification where technically and semantically appropriate.

HD already contains a Preserve-related building effect that can spread Biological Resources, including:

- Bonus;
- Luxury;
- Strategic biological Resources.

Therefore real Resource propagation is not inherently outside HD’s existing design language.

However the new Landscape action is more controllable and repeatable across multiple mother Resources.

So the mechanic remains:

> **HIGH-RISK / BALANCE & SYSTEM-INTERACTION TEST REQUIRED**

Important external systems include:

- duplicate Luxury trade;
- Industries;
- Corporations;
- Product generation;
- Monopoly-related Tourism effects;
- HD Resource-type / Resource-copy interactions;
- civilization-specific Resource abilities.

No Resource class is currently excluded purely on theory.

# 29. Design Story

The completed progression is:

### Lv1 — 风土护育
> Learn to use land without unnecessarily consuming its long-term landscape value.

### Lv2 — 地景营造
> Move from avoiding damage to actively shaping working land into landscape.

### Lv3 — 风物经营
> Turn high-quality landscape into recreation economy and establish professional long-term land maintenance.

### Lv4 — 国家景观统筹
> Build formal protected areas, expand management capacity, support surrounding managed landscapes, and cultivate biological-resource distribution through habitat management.

The specialization therefore evolves from:

> **care**
>
> → **creation**
>
> → **management**
>
> → **governance**

---

# 30. Operational Identity

Landscape’s active gameplay is deliberately:

> **low-frequency, map-based, long-term investment**

rather than high-frequency action spam.

The key player actions are:

### Maintain
Temporarily remove a working tile from labor use in exchange for permanent improvement.

### Allocate
Choose which land receives scarce Management Capacity.

### Protect
Convert appropriate land into formal National Parks.

### Arrange
Position protected cores relative to managed landscapes.

### Cultivate
Use advanced management capacity to encourage biological Resources to establish new distributions.

The player does not need:

- per-tile maturity meters;
- maintenance taxes;
- zoning quotas;
- Masterplan stances;
- complex adjacency taxonomies;
- carbon meters;
- high-frequency Builder micromanagement.

---

# 31. Main Design Principle

Landscape does not attempt to make:

> every action a difficult dilemma.

Some effects are:

> positive opportunities with timing and capacity costs.

Biological Resource propagation is intentionally one such system.

Its value lies partly in:

> keeping the city map dynamically relevant after initial development.

By contrast, Ecological Maintenance carries a more direct investment tradeoff:

> current land use vs future permanent land quality.

---

# 33. Remaining Rules / Boundary Questions

Still to finalize:

---

# 34. Implementation Feasibility Questions

Development should separately investigate:

- temporarily making a tile unworkable without rewriting its displayed yields;
- long-running tile-bound tasks;
- occupation and release of Management Capacity;
- interrupting excess tasks when capacity falls;
- training restrictions based on current capacity;
- persistent source-city binding of Landscape Management Teams;
- dynamic real Resource creation;
- Resource Terrain / Feature legality checks;
- persistent mother/child propagation eligibility;
- National Park range detection;
- non-stacking protected-buffer buffs.

Implementation limitations should be reported back to Design.

They should not silently redefine the gameplay contract.
